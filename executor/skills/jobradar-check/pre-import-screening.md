# Evidence-based pre-import screening

Apply the owner's configured hard constraints and any newer explicit correction from the owner. Keep personal constraints in private operational configuration, not this skill. A correction changes selection for remaining work, not the pinned source list, query, order, filters or limits. Record its application in the next checkpoint without rewriting previous results.

Screen the listing before opening a detail. Reject a clearly unrelated required primary stack, a proven incompatible attendance requirement, or a mandatory working language the owner cannot use. A mention of an additional technology is not proof of an unrelated primary stack. Generic backend/full-stack titles, optional technology choices and new projects still warrant detail review.

Compare required experience with the owner's evidenced experience and evaluate the substantive stack, not keyword overlap or a requirement for perfect coverage. A substantial core-stack match can pass screening with explicit gaps to verify; do not invent missing experience. Reject a clearly mandatory, non-substitutable experience requirement the owner does not meet. Distinguish mandatory recurring attendance from optional office visits; apply the owner's current attendance constraints even when the office is not abroad. A substantial stack match does not override a proven attendance or working-language blocker.

Screen again after reading a detail and before `import`. An automatically opened first detail is not an obligation to import. If a hard mismatch becomes clear only in the detail, do not create an opportunity, assessment or action item for it. No full translation is needed for a rejected candidate. Stop reading once evidence is sufficient, unless resolving an actual ambiguity requires more context.

Do not infer attendance from a distant company address alone. Distinguish a mandatory office/hybrid requirement from flexible or unspecified arrangements. Do not infer the working language solely from the language of the advertisement. Unknown remote eligibility, language, rate or flexibility remains unknown: a technically relevant candidate without a proven hard mismatch may be imported for verification, with the unresolved condition explicit. Never claim that translation assistance proves working-language eligibility.

Audit each rejection through `checkpoint`: stable public offer URL or portal ID, listing position, short evidence, reason category (stack, attendance, language), whether its detail was opened, and cumulative rejection count in `completed_unit`. Keep within the checkpoint size limit; use small batches rather than dropping evidence. Existing execution events retain earlier checkpoints. Do not send unsupported payload fields. Rejected listing positions still consume the applicable step budget; category linked results increment `step_related_count`, not the category-entry counter. Count automatically opened details too, even without an import. Carry the cumulative rejected count through recovery and into `finish.rejected_count`, without counting the same position twice.

Save a checkpoint after each processed detail, including rejected details, before moving on. A routine batch ends with `pause`, not terminal partial. A rejected candidate is not a source failure or proof that the rest of the search is empty. Existing opportunities and their decisions remain untouched, including when a rediscovered offer is now rejected by this gate.
